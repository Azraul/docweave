# Hardcore History — Prophets of Doom

**Host:** Dan Carlin  
**Episode:** Prophets of Doom  
**Series:** Hardcore History  
**Release:** ~2014  
**Runtime:** ~4.5 hours

Dan Carlin's *Prophets of Doom* tells the story of the **Münster Rebellion** (1534–1535), one of the strangest and most violent episodes of the Protestant Reformation.

### The Setup

In 1534, a group of radical Anabaptists — believing the apocalypse was imminent — took control of the city of Münster in modern-day Germany. They declared it the **New Jerusalem**, expelled anyone who wouldn't convert, and established a theocracy based on direct revelation from God.

Their leader, Jan Matthys, was a baker from Haarlem who believed he was a prophet. When he died charging a vastly larger army with only a handful of followers, leadership passed to **Jan van Leiden** — a tailor-turned-king who introduced polygamy, executed dissenters personally in the marketplace, and crowned himself "King of the New Israel."

### Why it's worth knowing

Carlin's genius is making you understand that these weren't cartoon villains. They were genuinely convinced God was on their side. The siege lasted 16 months. When it fell, the leaders were tortured to death with red-hot tongs, and their bodies were hung in cages from St. Lambert's Church — where the cages still hang today.

It's a story about:
- What happens when certainty meets power
- How apocalyptic thinking can reshape society overnight
- The uncomfortable similarities between 1534 and... well, pick any century
