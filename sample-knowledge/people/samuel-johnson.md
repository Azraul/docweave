# Samuel Johnson

**Samuel Johnson** (1709–1784) was an English writer, poet, essayist, and literary critic — best known for compiling *A Dictionary of the English Language* in 1755, a monumental work that single-handedly standardized English spelling and usage for generations.

He was also, famously, a tremendous wit. His put-downs and one-liners were legendary:

> "A woman's preaching is like a dog's walking on his hinder legs. It is not done well; but you are surprised to find it done at all."

> "Patriotism is the last refuge of a scoundrel."

> "When a man is tired of London, he is tired of life."

### 🫣 Blinking Sam

In 1772, Sir Joshua Reynolds painted a portrait of Johnson that captured him with a slightly bewildered, almost-confused expression. In 2012, this portrait resurfaced online as **"Blinking Sam"** — a reaction image used when someone reads something so absurd, so baffling, that words fail.

The typical caption:

> **"What the fuck did I just read?"**

The meme endures because Johnson's expression — half squint, half grimace — perfectly captures the sensation of encountering something incomprehensible on the internet. It has been deployed against cryptocurrency whitepapers, AI-generated recipes, political manifestos, and this very README.

### Why he's here

Samuel Johnson represents a fundamental truth about knowledge bases: **information lives forever, but context changes.** Johnson's dictionary was a serious work of scholarship. His bewildered face is now a reaction image. Both are valid entries in a knowledge base — the serious and the silly coexist.

He is also one of the most quoted Englishmen in history, which makes his own confused-meme status deeply ironic. Imagine writing half the dictionary and being remembered as "the blinking guy." He'd have a quip for it.

> *"No man but a blockhead ever wrote, except for money."*

Reynolds got his money. Johnson got his meme. This is the balance of history.
